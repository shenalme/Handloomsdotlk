01 / THE PROPOSITION

What happens when living craft becomes a digital research object?

This project brings together fashion design, handloom knowledge, digitalisation and international exchange to explore new pathways for Sri Lankan heritage textiles.

The research was developed collaboratively by the University of Moratuwa, Cardiff Metropolitan University and the University of Reading, with support from the British Council Connections Through Culture programme.

03 / EXPLORE THE HANDLOOMS

A fieldwork archive, not just a gallery.

The team worked across Central, North-western, Southern, Eastern and Northern provinces, combining participatory visits, observation, artisan profiling, interviews and sample development.

04 / THE COLLECTION

From scanned textile to six-outfit fashion range.

High-resolution handloom textile scans informed a six-outfit fashion range. AI-assisted concept generation was followed by design screening, technical review, prototype development, pattern manipulation, fabric manipulation and quality assurance.

01Scan

Handloom textiles were documented at high resolution.

02Translate

Patterns, colour and texture informed contemporary silhouettes.

03Prototype

Technical teams adapted concepts to the realities of handwoven cloth.

04Refine

Motif placement, shrinkage, drape, finishing and wearability were iteratively addressed.

05 / DIGITAL TWINS

The garment leaves the studio.
The digital twin stays accessible.

The project developed a web-based pipeline from physical garment preparation to photogrammetric reconstruction and browser-based interaction. The published models were designed for rotation, zoom and viewpoint changes.

06 / FIELDWORK

Five provinces.
Fifteen centres.

Fieldwork was organised through provincial industry departments and focused on primary data collection, artisan profiling, interviews, observation and sample manufacturing. The archive below makes the geographic and material journey visible.

PRACTICE-LED RESEARCH

Artisans were treated as knowledge holders and collaborators. Their lived experiences, skills, constraints and design possibilities formed the basis for later textile and fashion development.

07 / UK–SRI LANKA EXCHANGE

Sri Lanka → Cardiff → Wales → Reading → London

The project moved from fieldwork and collection-building into exhibitions, comparative visits and conversations about the future of craft.

01 · CARDIFF

Threaded Heritage

A three-day exhibition at Cardiff Metropolitan University brought physical handloom fabrics and garment prototypes together with digital twins and interactive experiences.

02 · WALES

Comparative craft visits

Visits to St Fagans, Elvet Wollen Mill / The London Cloth Co. and Melin Tregwynt opened a comparative conversation around wool, weaving, heritage, markets and digitalisation.

03 · READING

University showcase

The work continued at the University of Reading's Whiteknights Campus, connecting heritage, digital innovation, accessibility and cross-cultural exchange.

04 · LONDON · 22 SEP 2025

Craft beyond the exhibition

The Sri Lanka High Commission in London hosted a round table on Sri Lankan crafts, bringing together academics, experts, entrepreneurs, retailers, writers, students and craft enthusiasts.

09 / THE PROJECT

Empowering Handloom Artistry:
A Digital Approach to Globalize the Heritage Handloom Industry in Sri Lanka

A collaborative research project funded through the British Council Connections Through Culture programme.

The project combined Sri Lankan handloom fieldwork and fashion expertise with UK digitalisation and accessibility expertise. It explored how artisan knowledge, textile sampling, contemporary design and digital technologies could work together to expand the reach of heritage handloom.
